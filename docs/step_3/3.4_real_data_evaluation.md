# Step 3.4 — Real-Data OCR Evaluation

## Scope

The assignment-provided documents were processed as real OCR inputs.

They are deliberately **not** treated as synthetic ground truth.

Two documents were evaluated:

| Document | Pages |
|---|---:|
| `DocScanner Apr 21_ 2026 6-48 PM.pdf` | 22 |
| `MP22IGR17182025A100906565 (1).pdf` | 31 |

## Observable evidence

Current OCR output reports include:

| Document | Characters | Words | Mean confidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DocScanner | 3,087 | 616 | 0.5329 |
| MP22IGR | 10,990 | 1,991 | 0.6404 |

These are observable OCR-quality signals.

## Ground-truth limitation

Authoritative transcriptions and critical-field labels were not available for the assignment documents.

Therefore the project does **not** claim:

- CER/WER for the assignment documents
- field-level accuracy for the assignment documents
- an authoritative OCR accuracy percentage

Instead, the real-data evaluation reports:

- page counts
- character/word counts
- confidence distributions
- low-confidence pages/regions
- near-empty pages
- runtime information

## Methodological boundary

This separation prevents a common evaluation error: presenting an OCR engine's output as both the prediction and the ground truth.
